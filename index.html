Abstract

From a low-cost walk-through to a simulation-ready building model

Building Energy Digital Twins need structured, simulation-ready representations that integrate geometry, topology, semantics, thermal properties and energy-related systems. For existing buildings these are still produced largely by hand, especially the identification, 3D localization and parameterization of energy-relevant devices. We present a mapping-to-BIM/BEM workflow that generates simulation-ready Building Energy Models directly from low-cost mobile mapping data. Structural geometry and spatial organization are reconstructed from 3D point clouds, while energy-related devices are detected in registered multi-view imagery and consolidated into unique 3D instances through cross-view semantic and geometric consistency. Technical information on device nameplates is read with OCR and attached to the corresponding physical instance. Spaces, devices and attributes are integrated into a typed semantic graph, which is translated into EnergyPlus epJSON and a BIM environment.

1 · Introduction

Why automate the path from survey to energy model

Europe’s building stock is ageing and inefficient, and scaling retrofit needs reliable digitalization. Today, energy-relevant components are still surveyed by experts on site and modelled by hand.

Building Energy Models (BEMs) are the simulation-active layer of Energy Digital Twins: watertight B-Rep geometry enriched with semantics, topology, materials, systems and operational data. Creating them remains poorly automated, especially locating and characterising energy-related devices.

Low-cost indoor mobile mapping gives spatially referenced images, camera poses and point clouds; visual AI can identify, localize and structure what they contain. This work builds on our Scan-to-EDT workflow and turns raw data from the STELVIO system into simulation-ready energy models, a first step towards Energy Digital Twins once sensor data are connected.

1.1 Aim of the study: one integrated workflow
  • building geometry is reconstructed from 3D data;
  • energy-relevant devices are detected in multi-view imagery and persistently localized in 3D;
  • technical parameters are extracted from imagery;
  • all information is linked to rooms, surfaces and thermal zones through a typed semantic graph.

3 · Methodology

Six stages from raw observations to an energy-oriented building graph

(i) data acquisition, (ii) structural 3D reconstruction, (iii) image-based device recognition, (iv) multi-view instance reconstruction, (v) technical attribute extraction and (vi) graph-based BEM generation.

  1. Data acquisitionSTELVIO (stereo-visual-LiDAR-inertial) outputs a dense RGB point cloud, the camera trajectory and raw imagery in one SLAM frame.
  2. Structural 3D reconstructionSuperpoint Transformer labels floor, ceiling, wall, column and openings; RANSAC, DBSCAN and wall-plane rasterization split them into instances.
  3. Image-based device recognitionOpen-vocabulary detectors and segmenters find lights, radiators, sockets, sensors and HVAC units from class prompts.
  4. Multi-view 3D instance reconstructionMasks are back-projected with depth and pose; cross-view support votes each point's class and geometric priors remove implausible instances.
  5. Technical attribute extractionGLM-OCR reads nameplates into parameter–value–unit tuples, linked to the device, its room and thermal zone.
  6. Graph-based BEM generationSpaces, elements, surfaces and devices form a typed semantic graph, exported to EnergyPlus epJSON and to Revit.

3.1STELVIO mobile mapping acquisition

The in-house STELVIO system combines stereo cameras, LiDAR and an IMU in a factor-graph framework. It outputs dense RGB point clouds, the camera trajectory and raw imagery in the SLAM frame: the basis for image-to-3D reprojection and multi-view instance reconstruction.

3.2Structural 3D reconstruction

1 · Semantic labellingSuperpoint Transformer, pretrained on ScanNet, classifies floor, ceiling, wall, column, opening and other; a PCA-normal fallback covers missing inference.
2 · Instance segmentationFloors and ceilings by elevation clustering and DBSCAN; walls by iterative RANSAC; columns by linearity and verticality; openings refined by rasterization and flood fill in the wall plane.
3 · Panoptic encodingEach element gets a unique identifier of class and instance; every point stores labels, colour, normal and local geometric descriptors.

3.3Image processing: energy device recognition

Devices are found in registered RGB images with class-specific synonym prompts. Four strategies are compared: A, B and D are detector-first; C is segment-first.

3.4Multi-view agreement and 3D instance reconstruction

Mask pixels are back-projected with depth, intrinsics and pose. For each 3D point p, the support for class c counts the views in which p is visible and falls inside a mask of c. A point takes the class with the highest support only above a minimum number of views; labelled points are grouped into device instances and filtered by height, wall proximity, extent and mask area.

Sc(p) = Σi=1…N Ii(p, c)

3.5Instance validation in 3D and geometric refinement

Predicted instances are matched one-to-one to manual references; localization and semantic correctness are scored separately, and matched instances are refined with the fused multi-view observations.

3.6Extraction of device technical parameters

GLM-OCR reads equipment nameplates; text is parsed into parameter–value–unit tuples (rated power, voltage, flow rate, model…), normalized to EnergyPlus conventions and written to the matching epJSON fields. Each tuple stays linked to its 3D device, room and thermal zone.

3.7Solid model and semantic graph for spatial reasoning

The reconstructed geometry becomes a 3D solid model, then volumetric and surface-based topological representations and the corresponding graph, exported as BIM and as an EnergyPlus BEM. The graph links geometry, topology, semantics, devices and energy information in one multi-level digital twin structure.

4 · Results

Evaluation on ScanNet

Recognition and structural reconstruction are benchmarked on ScanNet (312 scans); OCR and the complete workflow are assessed on self-acquired data.

4.1Image-based semantic recognition

At image level, Method C detects 73.4% of devices at 49.5% precision; A and B are the most precise (79.3%, 78.6%) but recall about 35%. At instance level, after geometric filtering, A reaches 54.0% F1 (P 56.6%, R 51.7%), followed by B (51.7%), C (49.7%) and D (42.6%). Remaining errors come from background similarity, small or visually variable objects and taxonomy mismatch.

4.2Structural 3D reconstruction

All 312 scans were processed. Floor, ceiling and wall reach 87–94% IoU; openings and columns remain the weakest classes (46–59%).

4.3Multi-view 3D reconstruction and computational performance

Method D is the most balanced (0.66 s/frame, 4.66 instances/s, about 3.56 GB); A has the lowest memory (2.29 GB). Recognition and fusion take about 6 min per scene on a laptop RTX 4050 GPU.

4.4Semantic graph and BEM representation

Nodes are spatial, architectural and device entities; edges encode verified spatial or functional relations. Each device is linked to its room, supporting surface and thermal zone.

4.5Image-to-epJSON device parameter enrichment

Across seven boiler and water-heater nameplates, 48 of 52 energy-relevant parameters (92%) were extracted correctly; most errors came from one low-resolution, strongly inclined plate.

4.6Energy simulations

Each zone was simulated in EnergyPlus 24.2 with generic constructions, the Mannheim (DWD) weather file and ideal loads (20/26 °C). Volume error follows the footprint (correlation 0.87), not the height (0.04): improving footprint closure in partly scanned rooms is the most effective next step.

6 · Conclusions

An end-to-end, low-cost path from mapping to BIM and BEM

The workflow integrates structural reconstruction, image-based device recognition, multi-view 3D instance reconstruction, OCR-based parameter extraction and semantic graph generation, with export to BIM and EnergyPlus.

The best configuration (YOLO-World + SAM2) reaches 54.0% instance-level F1; GroundingDINO + SAM2 (42.6%) offers the best runtime–memory balance. Main structural classes reach about 90% IoU, 311 of 312 ScanNet scans give a valid EnergyPlus model, and OCR extracts 92% of parameters correctly.

Limitations and next stepsFalse positives, duplicate or missed instances, occlusions, small or ambiguous devices, taxonomy mismatch and nameplate readability. Future work: stronger false-positive suppression, duplicate merging, domain-specific recognition and validation across more building types.

Thumbnail

The six views

All five 3D views share one camera. The point clouds are random subsets of about 45,000 points per scan with the ceiling removed. Walls, doors and windows come from the reconstructed model, and the surfaces are the ones written to the EnergyPlus input file.

Metrics

Footprint IoU: overlap of the reconstructed zone footprint with the reference footprint. Floor-area, height and volume error: signed relative error against the reference. Wall distance: median distance of wall-labelled points to their reconstructed wall plane.

Energy

Each zone was simulated in EnergyPlus 24.2 with generic constructions, the Mannheim (DWD) weather file and ideal loads at 20/26 °C. Heating and cooling are annual zone loads; the per-m² values divide by the reconstructed floor area.
